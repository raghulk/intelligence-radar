# Dhaga & Co. | Phase 1 Discovery Note

**Date:** 4 October 2026 | **Stage:** Discovery, before MVP implementation

## 1. The Problem

Neha cannot identify which vendors and sizes need fixing because she can only read a fraction of customers' return explanations.

## 2. Owner and Current Approach

**Neha, Category Head**, owns the return-rate problem. She hand-reads a few hundred "Other" reasons at a time; reviews are displayed but never analysed.

## 3. Evidence

- **31% returns:** Neha's manual reading suggests most Other reasons concern fit. (Client brief, Section 05: Neha.)
- **44% Other; 410,000 unread reviews:** return explanations and 18 months of feedback remain largely unused. (Section 04: Returns and Reviews.)
- **Vendor size charts differ; orders are clean:** existing data could support vendor/category analysis, subject to join validation. (Section 04: Catalogue and Orders.)

## 4. What It Costs Today

If 31% applies to **48,000 placed orders/week**, it implies **14,880 returns** and about **6,550 Other reasons**. These are derived workload estimates, not fit-only losses. Confirm the return-rate denominator, fit share and processing cost. (Sections 02, 04 and 05.)

## 5. What Success Looks Like

- **Usable reasons:** target 90%+, versus 56% non-Other today; verify existing labels and report unresolved cases.
- **Classification quality:** target 85%+ agreement on accepted labels against Neha's checked sample; report acceptance coverage.
- **Business outcome:** lower fit-return rates after corrective action, measured using returns and delivered items in comparable vendor/category cohorts.

Proposed MVP: a weekly fit breakdown with supporting evidence to help Neha choose corrective actions. Targets are proposed acceptance criteria, not achieved results.

## 6. Ranked Shortlist and Why This Comes First

Ranking weighs impact, evidence/data readiness, language-work suitability, operability, risk and existing alternatives.

| Rank | Problem / Owner | Reason for Position |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Fit-return diagnosis / Neha | High return rate; unused reasons/reviews provide a near-term test of vendor fit problems |
| 2 | COD return-to-origin / Faizan | INR 4.75 crore/year logistics, derived from stated volumes and INR 120/RTO; cause mix needs investigation |
| 3 | Order-status tickets / Arpita | 58% of tickets; explore existing status lookup and notifications first |
| 4 | Slow product listing / Vivek | 6-9 days; actual bottleneck and revenue impact unknown |
| 5 | Analytics request backlog / Karthik | Two-week queue; focused return analysis offers a narrower first step |

Fit comes first because potential cause evidence already exists. RTO has the clearest monetary cost, but needs cause investigation before choosing an intervention.

## 7. Biggest Assumption and How to Test It

**Assumption:** fit explains a substantial share of Other returns and clusters by vendor/category/size. Test with a Neha-labelled random sample and cohort rates. If fit is uncommon or patterns are not actionable, reconsider priority; COD RTO is the next candidate.
